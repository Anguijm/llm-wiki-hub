# Use a four-step calendar-audit-to-skill pipeline to systematically delegate recurring tasks

## Hypothesis

If we identify our top three most time-consuming recurring tasks via calendar audit and apply a four-step process (one-shot execution, let Claude generate the skill, run from skill, iterate and update), then we will delegate those tasks reliably because skills capture context, edge cases, and process knowledge discovered during the initial messy back-and-forth rather than requiring us to write instructions from scratch.

## What they did

Shaw taught a framework he uses in 1-on-1 founder coaching sessions. Step 1: audit your calendar for the last two weeks and identify the top three recurring time sinks. Step 2: run the task with Claude in a single conversation, iterating until output quality is acceptable (15–30 min). Step 3: tell Claude to generate a reusable skill from the conversation — Claude distills context, steps, and corrections into a skill file. Step 4: open a fresh conversation, invoke the skill, and observe it completing the task in ~3 minutes. Skills are treated as living documents updated incrementally as new edge cases appear, rather than designed exhaustively upfront.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Provides a concrete process for bootstrapping new YOLO loop skills. Instead of writing skill files by hand, we can discover them organically through an initial exploratory session and then crystallize them, lowering the barrier to adding new automated steps.
