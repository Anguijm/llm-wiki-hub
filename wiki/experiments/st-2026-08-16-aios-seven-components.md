## Hypothesis

If we explicitly map our development workflow to the seven AIOS components (people, agents, applications, connectors, instructions, records, automations), then we will identify gaps and redundancies in our current tooling and process coverage because the framework forces explicit accounting of every element needed for agent-driven work.

## What they did

Shaw defined an AI Operating System as the combination of people, AI agents, applications, connectors, instructions, records, and automations organized to get work done. He walked through his own AIOS: using Claude CoWork (~80% of agent usage), Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, and Gemini as agents; Google Suite and Notion as primary apps; MCP servers as connectors; SOPs stored in Notion as instructions; CRM, contracts, metrics, and strategy docs as records; and time- or event-triggered Claude CoWork sessions as automations. He then demonstrated three daily automations: morning email triage, content pipeline management, and client engagement CRM sync — each producing a skimmable summary with flagged action items requiring only 15-30 minutes of human review.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Directly applicable: we can audit the YOLO loop against each of the seven components to confirm we have explicit owners for instructions (SOPs/prompts), records (project state, experiment cards), connectors (MCP/API integrations), and automations (scheduled or event-triggered agent runs), and surface any missing layer.
