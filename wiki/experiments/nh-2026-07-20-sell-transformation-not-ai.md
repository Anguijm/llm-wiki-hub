## Hypothesis

If we present AI development tooling demos as concrete transformation narratives (specific problem → specific measurable outcome) rather than capability showcases, then stakeholder buy-in and adoption rates will increase, because people respond to stories about impact rather than technology descriptions.

## What they did

Nate B. Jones argued that the core failure of AI vendors and agency builders is selling AI capabilities instead of selling the transformation and real-world impact. He used the analogy that handing out Claude credits without context is like giving a live 480-volt line to a factory worker and saying 'figure it out.' He recommended always anchoring the pitch to the specific business story and measurable change, and noted that CEO-level adoption requires leaders to visibly use the tools themselves to model the behaviour they want.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Relevant when documenting or pitching YOLO loop experiments internally: framing each experiment card outcome as a transformation story (what changed, by how much) rather than a technical description will increase team engagement and prioritisation of high-impact experiments.
