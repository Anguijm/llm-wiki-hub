# Apply wound/click/transformation framework to AI tool documentation and onboarding copy

## Hypothesis

If we rewrite internal and external AI tool descriptions using the wound-click-transformation structure (identify the pain moment, anchor to a known story, show before/after), then more stakeholders will immediately understand and adopt the tool, because abstract AI jargon creates no mental image and no emotional connection, while a concrete pain moment plus visible transformation lets people self-identify as the target user.

## What they did

Veronica presented a three-part messaging framework for AI products. Step 1 (Wound): open with the user's painful daily moment, not what you built—immerse the audience in the frustration before naming the solution, in ~20 seconds identifying the wound, saying 'we fix that,' then showing how. Step 2 (Click): make the product mechanism instantly graspable by a non-expert, either by anchoring to a viral reference the audience already understands (e.g., 'if McDonald's had used us, that AI drive-thru never would have made it to TikTok') or by using a concrete metaphor ('smoke alarm for AI behavior', 'Devon the AI software engineer') instead of category jargon. Step 3 (Transformation): show a specific before/after rather than abstract benefit claims—'before: 30 minutes digging through docs; after: one question, answer in 10 seconds with sources.' She argued that in the current market, products with the clearest stories get funded, bought, and talked about, while great tech with opaque descriptions dies quietly.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Applies to how we document and pitch the yolo-loop itself to internal stakeholders or in READMEs. Running the wound-click-transformation pass on loop documentation or experiment cards could increase adoption and stakeholder buy-in without any engineering changes.
