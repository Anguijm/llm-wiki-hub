## Hypothesis

If we audit a business by asking 'what would break first if we doubled customers tomorrow?' rather than 'what is annoying?', then the automations we build will remove real bottlenecks and generate measurable ROI, because attacking constraints grows the business whereas automating annoyances only saves marginal time.

## What they did

Nate described a four-step roadmap for becoming an in-house AI consultant: (1) audit your own workflows for automation opportunities, (2) build and ship small internal automations to prove value, (3) run a constraint-first audit of the whole business to find the real bottleneck, (4) quantify hours/dollars saved into a single number and propose a formal AI role to management. He cited IBM data showing chief AI officer roles grew from 26% to 76% of large companies in ~2 years, and that AI-skilled workers earn ~56% more than peers.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Useful framing for prioritising which parts of our own dev loop to automate first — focus on the constraint that would cause the whole pipeline to fail under scale, not just the most annoying manual step.

## Notes

More career/org advice than pure technical experiment; relevance is in prioritisation methodology.
