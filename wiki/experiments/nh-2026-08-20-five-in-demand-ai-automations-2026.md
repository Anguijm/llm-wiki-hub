# Build a domain-specific lead qualification workflow with human handoff and measurable CRM output

> Back to [[experiments-index]]

**Status:** `backlog` · **Effort:** `medium`

## Hypothesis

If we build a lead qualification agent scoped to a single vertical (e.g., HVAC) with explicit scoring rules, CRM record creation, and a clear human handoff for low-confidence cases, then we will have a demonstrably measurable automation that a domain buyer will trust more than a generic multi-vertical demo.

## What they did

Nate Herk analyzed recent surveys, marketplaces, job listings, and case studies to rank the five most in-demand AI automations. He described lead qualification and follow-up as the top automation: enrich the lead, score it against company-specific rules, update CRM, route by territory, and provide the salesperson a score explanation. He cited Salesforce data showing Siemens deploying this across 2,800 inbound leads/week with 50 verification rules. He emphasized picking one vertical, using one reliable workflow (not multi-agent), and tracking response time and qualified-lead-to-booking rate. He argued that the 50 verification rules are where the real value lives.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Maps to building a focused, testable agent pipeline: single vertical, defined rules, measurable output metric, human-in-the-loop for edge cases. This is a concrete portfolio project pattern that can validate our agent workflow architecture.

## Notes

Other four automations ranked: (2) customer support resolution with reopen-rate tracking, (3) voice AI receptionist for missed/after-hours calls, (4) document processing, (5) employee service and onboarding. Nate's meta-point: one relevant, domain-specific project beats 30 generic demos. Measure what matters: for support, track correct resolutions AND reopen rate.
