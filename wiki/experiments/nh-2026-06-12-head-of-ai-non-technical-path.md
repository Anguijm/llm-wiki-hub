## Hypothesis

If a non-technical practitioner stays hands-on with Claude Code for daily implementation work while also owning cross-company AI strategy, then they will be more effective at identifying automatable processes and maintaining credibility with technical staff because they can directly evaluate what the tools produce rather than relying on second-hand reports.

## What they did

Nate interviewed Eileen, who went from zero AI experience to Head of AI across 15 companies in one year. She described her role as owning AI strategy for a parent company and 15 subsidiaries while remaining personally hands-on with Claude Code implementations. Her workflow: map a business process with stakeholders, decide what should and should not be automated, then implement directly in Claude Code. She cited an IBM survey showing Chief AI Officer equivalents jumped from 26% to 76% of CEOs in 24 months, and a utilization gap (85% of employees have AI skills but only ~25% actual utilization) as the core problem her role addresses. She is actively hiring from Nate's community and emphasized staying hands-on because the space changes daily.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Relevant to how we staff and scope the YOLO loop's human-in-the-loop roles. The process-mapping-before-automating step and the explicit 'what should not be automated' decision gate are practices we can encode as pre-sprint checklists before adding new automation targets to the backlog.

## Notes

Less a technical experiment and more an org/process signal. The utilization gap stat (85% skill, 25% use) is a useful benchmark for measuring our own internal adoption after deploying YOLO loop tooling.

Backlog triage 2026-06-24 (owner-preference model). Career/role validation — not a technical experiment.
