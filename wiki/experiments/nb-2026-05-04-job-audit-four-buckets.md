# Run a Four-Bucket Work Audit to Identify AI-Vulnerable Task Categories

## Hypothesis

If we categorize all tasks from the past two weeks into Theater, Commodity, On-the-Line, and Durable buckets, then we can identify which parts of the dev/ops workflow are already automatable by AI and which represent compounding human value, because the audit reveals where effort is spent on work that no longer requires a person versus work where judgment is irreplaceable.

## What they did

The speaker described a personal work audit framework that sorts every task from the past week into four buckets: Theater (visible busyness with little real output), Commodity (routine repeatable work AI can already do), On-the-Line (work at risk as AI capability grows), and Durable (work requiring human judgment, relationships, or novel problem-solving). He argued that the useful question is not 'will AI replace me' but 'how much of my last two weeks still needed me,' and recommended feeding one's calendar into Claude or Codex to help categorize and build a plan around the results.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Directly applicable to auditing which steps in the YOLO dev loop (e.g., writing summaries, formatting outputs, routing tasks, generating boilerplate) are already commodity-automatable versus which steps require human judgment about architecture, prioritization, or novel problem framing. The audit output could inform where to invest agentic automation next.
