## Hypothesis

If we evaluate stalled or ruled-out projects through the lens of taste, agency, focus, resourcefulness, and follow-through, then we will identify which ones are now viable with AI assistance and avoid over-pruning our opportunity space based on outdated capability assumptions.

## What they did

The speaker argued that five human skills—taste (acting on what you care about and noticing distinctions others skip), agency (doing something rather than writing a memo), focus (sustained attention on a specific problem), resourcefulness (learning what you need along the way), and follow-through (carrying a result all the way to real-world impact rather than stopping at an impressive first version)—have increased in leverage as AI has made more of the work between idea and outcome accessible. He walked through each skill with concrete examples: using AI to build an interactive onboarding guide instead of filing a complaint memo, using AI to prototype a solution so taste becomes precise, and staying involved through feedback cycles after shipping. He closed by recommending listeners audit their backlog with this skill group before planning for 2027.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Directly maps to our loop's planning and prioritization phase: the framework is a lightweight decision filter for which experiments to pull from backlog into active development, and the follow-through emphasis reinforces our loop's requirement to close experiments with a real verdict rather than stopping at a working prototype.
