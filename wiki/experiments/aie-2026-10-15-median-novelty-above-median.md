# Apply the 'novelty forcing questions' framework before starting any new agent-built feature to screen for above-median differentiation

## Hypothesis

If we gate each new feature or project with three forcing questions — what value does this provide that open models alone don't, what does this team know that others don't, and where will this live with distribution — then we avoid shipping median-quality AI products that have no durability in open competition, because the speaker found prompt-based median products are useful but fragile without a genuine novelty moat.

## What they did

Hari argued that the deterministic software stack (Kafka, Kubernetes, etc.) running the world economy is unchanged by AI — AI accelerates writing it but doesn't replace it. He spent two years building what he called 'the median': AI-assisted products that work but offer no durable differentiation. He built an open-source calculator (244 options evaluated) for comparing open-weight model deployment costs (self-hosted vs. cloud tokens) because no existing tool covered that specific problem. He outlined three novelty forcing questions to apply before building: (1) What value above open models does this provide? (2) What does our team know that others don't? (3) Where will this live/what's the distribution mode? He concluded that novelty is scarce and the median is free.
