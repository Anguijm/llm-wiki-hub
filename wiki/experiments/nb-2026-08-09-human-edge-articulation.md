# Define and Communicate Explicit Human-Owned Responsibilities in AI Rollout Docs

> Back to [[experiments-index]]

**Status:** `backlog` · **Effort:** `low`

## Hypothesis

If the AI rollout plan explicitly lists tasks that remain human-owned (eval writing, strategy alignment, removing LLMisms, system design), then engineers will engage more productively with AI tooling because they have a concrete vision of their evolving role rather than an ambiguous threat.

## What they did

Speaker argued that when scaling from pilot to production, teams must communicate: the pilot's customer impact, why AI agents are being added, the long-term agent vision, security safeguards, and critically—what is 'sacred' and human-maintained. He named coding evals, editorial quality passes, strategic alignment, and system design as the durable human contributions even as agents handle coding tasks, iteration acceleration, and deep analysis.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Maps to documenting the human review layer in our YOLO loop—explicitly writing down what the human reviewer is responsible for (correctness of intent, architectural decisions, security review) reduces ambiguity and keeps engineers engaged rather than disengaged.
