## Hypothesis

If we identify whether our project is supply-constrained or demand-constrained and then build the AI system that attacks that specific bottleneck, then AI investments will produce measurable growth impact rather than incremental time savings, because growth is capped by the single largest bottleneck and removing it directly unlocks the next stage.

## What they did

Nate Herk laid out a three-phase roadmap for becoming an in-house AI person. Phase 1 is positioning (pick one team, make work visible by asking what wastes the most time). Phase 2 is proving value (start with the most expensive, most repeatable, lowest-risk task—reports, inbox sorting, data cleaning—and attach real numbers to each win). Phase 3 is constraint-attack: diagnose whether the organization is supply-constrained (can't deliver fast enough) or demand-constrained (not enough customers), then build the AI system that attacks that bottleneck specifically. He argued that shaving hours off tasks is useful but doesn't grow a business; attacking the binding constraint does, and doing it repeatedly is what makes the role irreplaceable.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Applies directly to deciding which automations to build in the dev loop. Instead of building whatever is easiest, use the supply/demand constraint diagnostic to prioritize the automation that unblocks the actual growth ceiling.

## Notes

The constraint-attack framing is derived from Theory of Constraints. The quick diagnostic: ask 'what breaks if we doubled customers tomorrow?' If everything falls over = supply constrained. If we can handle it fine = demand constrained. Simple to run as a pre-build exercise.
