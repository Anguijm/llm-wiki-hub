# Adopt Lightweight 'Campfire' Async Collaboration Model for Distributed AI Dev Teams

## Hypothesis

If we replace mandatory co-location with opt-in 'campfire' gatherings tied to real customer or conference events, then engineering productivity and team trust will improve without forcing relocation, because spontaneous in-person clusters around meaningful work create stronger bonding signals than scheduled all-hands.

## What they did

Simon Eskildsen described Turbopuffer's fully distributed team structure. Rather than requiring people to be in SF or NYC, they run two structured company offsites per year plus informal 'campfires': any time a few people congregate in a city for a conference or customer meetings, they declare a campfire and invite the whole company optionally. They incentivize attendance and knowledge-sharing with 'turbo credits' redeemable for business-class flight upgrades. Some engineers attend every campfire; others only attend the twice-yearly offsite and stay home otherwise. The model explicitly accommodates both extremes.

## Relevance to YOLO loop

Our dev loop is async-first; formalizing a campfire policy (declare gatherings, make attendance optional, attach small incentives) could increase spontaneous collaboration around demos or conference sprints without disrupting deep-work default mode.

## Notes

Turbo credits idea is lightweight and humorous but reportedly effective at changing behavior. The 'central bank / interest rates / betting market' extensions of turbo credits emerging organically from the team suggest high engagement. Could adapt the incentive structure to any async team metric.
